Summary and Reflection

This project compared MIT subject data extracted from a 1996 catalog source and
the current MIT online catalog endpoint. The analysis shows substantial shifts
in department-level offerings, title vocabulary, and subject-code inventories.

1. Major departmental shifts
- The largest increase in this extraction was Course 20 (from 1 to 86, +85).
- The largest decrease after crosswalk normalization was 6 (from 206 to
  68, -138), with additional major decreases in Courses 15, 4, 1, and 12.
- These deltas likely mix real curriculum change with renumbering and
  extraction-coverage differences.
- The top reduced-department list now includes per-department reliability
  notes to separate strong vs caution-heavy interpretations.

2. Terminology changes in titles
- Rising terms included biology, systems, energy, data, learning, climate,
  computational, and engineering.
- Metadata-heavy legacy terms were reduced by stronger filtering. Declining
  terms are now dominated more by legacy catalog descriptors (for example
  seminar, advanced, theory, topics) than by connector words.
- A dedicated "likely format-artifact terms" section is now reported to keep
  formatting residue distinct from domain-topic decline interpretation.
- The lexical trend still indicates stronger emphasis on computational,
  life-science, and interdisciplinary themes in the modern catalog.

3. New and discontinued subjects
- Discontinued since 1996 (raw code-set difference): 1932
- New by 2024 (raw code-set difference): 1128
- A crosswalk-adjusted view is reported alongside raw counts to separate true
  change from renumbering effects.

4. Curriculum breadth interpretation
- Unique title terms:
  - 1996: 1535
  - 2024: 1171
- Entropy:
  - 1996: 9.0978
  - 2024: 8.8768
- On this token-distribution metric, 2024 appears slightly more specialized
  than the 1996 snapshot.

5. Data quality and extraction coverage
- MIT 1996 extracted records: 2625
- Description non-empty coverage: 1806 / 2625 (68.80%)
- OCR fallback was used on hard pages to improve text recovery.
- Mixed extraction sources (PDF text + OCR) require cautious interpretation
  for noisy legacy formatting lines.

Data quality note
- OCR runtime is active (`tesseract` available), and OCR fallback is integrated
  into extraction for image-heavy pages.

Limitations and next-step recommendation
- Department-level deltas can reflect renumbering/organizational restructuring,
  not only true expansion or shrinkage.
- Declining title terms still include likely legacy-format artifacts, so
  lexical decline should be interpreted with caution.
- MIT 1996 extraction mixes PDF text and OCR fallback; edge cases may remain
  and require manual spot checks.
- Next round should strengthen historical crosswalk evidence for large negative
  deltas while keeping current pipeline consistency checks.
