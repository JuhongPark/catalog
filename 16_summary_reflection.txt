Summary and Reflection

This project compared MIT subject data extracted from a 1996 catalog source and
the current MIT online catalog endpoint. The analysis shows large shifts in
department-level offerings, title vocabulary, and subject-code inventories.

1. Major departmental shifts
- The largest increase in this extraction was Course 20 (from 1 to 86, +85).
- The largest decrease was Course 6 (from 280 to 4, -276), with other major
  decreases in Courses 15, 18, 4, 8, and 1.
- These deltas likely mix real curriculum change with renumbering and
  historical catalog structure differences.

2. Terminology changes in titles
- Rising terms included biology, biological, science, energy, data, learning,
  climate, computational, machine, and ethics.
- Declining terms were dominated by metadata-like tokens (for example prereq,
  permission, spring, fall), plus broad terms such as seminar and theory.
- The lexical trend indicates stronger emphasis on computational and
  interdisciplinary themes in the modern catalog.

3. New and discontinued subjects
- Discontinued since 1996 (code-set difference): 1956
- New by 2024 (code-set difference): 1029
- The scale of change supports the interpretation that MIT's catalog has seen
  substantial restructuring over time.

4. Curriculum breadth interpretation
- Unique title terms:
  - 1996: 3028
  - 2024: 1140
- Entropy:
  - 1996: 9.9428
  - 2024: 8.8623
- On this token-distribution metric, 2024 appears more specialized than 1996.

5. Connection to broader trends
- Growth of terms such as data, learning, computational, and climate aligns
  with AI/data-intensive work, sustainability priorities, and
  interdisciplinary engineering.
- Increased life-science vocabulary aligns with biotechnology and health-tech
  expansion.

Data quality note
- 1996 description coverage improved versus early rounds, but OCR-backed
  cleanup is still not integrated. Some comparisons remain sensitive to
  extraction noise and formatting artifacts.
