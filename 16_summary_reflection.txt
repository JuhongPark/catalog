Summary and Reflection

This project compared MIT subject data extracted from a 1996 catalog source and
the current online catalog (2024/2026 listing endpoint). The analysis indicates
major shifts in both department-level offerings and title vocabulary.

1. Major departmental shifts
- Largest increase remained Course 20 (+85), while many other departments showed
  apparent decreases in this extraction snapshot.
- The largest decrease was Course 6 (-276), likely affected by
  renumbering/restructuring effects and extraction-format differences across eras.

2. Terminology changes in titles
- Rising terms included: biology, science, engineering, design, materials,
  data, systems, learning, climate, and computational.
- Declining terms included: special, problems, seminar, advanced, theory, and
  management-oriented labels in this extraction output.
- Overall, the vocabulary trend points toward modern computational and
  interdisciplinary themes.

3. New and discontinued subjects
- Discontinued since 1996: 2784 subject codes (by direct code set difference).
- New by 2024: 852 subject codes.
- These large numbers indicate substantial catalog restructuring, renumbering,
  and expansion over time.

4. Curriculum breadth interpretation
- Unique title terms:
  - 1996: 3028
  - 2024: 1140
- Entropy:
  - 1996: 9.9428
  - 2024: 8.8623
- By this metric, 2024 appears more specialized than 1996.

5. Link to education/industry trends
- The rise of terms like data, learning, computational, and climate aligns with
  industry demand in AI/data-intensive fields, sustainability, and
  cross-disciplinary engineering.
- Growth in life-science-related terms also aligns with biotechnology and
  health-tech expansion.

Data quality note
- 1996 extraction in this round improved description capture from PDF text, but
  OCR-grade cleanup is still not applied. Comparisons may still include noise
  from formatting and historical catalog structure differences.
