Summary and Reflection

This project compared MIT subject data extracted from a 1996 catalog source and
the current MIT online catalog endpoint. The analysis shows substantial shifts
in department-level offerings, title vocabulary, and subject-code inventories.

1. Major departmental shifts
- The largest increase in this extraction was Course 20 (from 1 to 86, +85).
- The largest decrease after crosswalk normalization was 6_EECS (from 199 to 4,
  -195), with additional major decreases in Courses 15, 4, 1, and 12.
- These deltas likely mix real curriculum change with renumbering and
  extraction-coverage differences.

2. Terminology changes in titles
- Rising terms included biology, biological, science, energy, data, systems,
  climate, learning, and computational.
- Metadata-heavy legacy terms were reduced by stronger filtering; however,
  declining terms still include connector words (for example in, of, or) and
  broad descriptors such as seminar and theory.
- The lexical trend still indicates stronger emphasis on computational,
  life-science, and interdisciplinary themes in the modern catalog.

3. New and discontinued subjects
- Discontinued since 1996 (raw code-set difference): 1926
- New by 2024 (raw code-set difference): 1034
- A crosswalk-adjusted view is now reported to separate true change from
  renumbering effects.

4. Curriculum breadth interpretation
- Unique title terms:
  - 1996: 1330
  - 2024: 1140
- Entropy:
  - 1996: 8.9383
  - 2024: 8.8623
- On this token-distribution metric, 2024 appears slightly more specialized
  than the 1996 snapshot.

5. Data quality and extraction confidence
- MIT 1996 extracted records: 2560
- Description non-empty coverage: 1739 / 2560 (67.93%)
- Average extraction confidence: 0.611
- Per-record quality flags now identify low-confidence rows for downstream
  filtering and review.

Data quality note
- OCR fallback logic is implemented in `10_extract_1996.py`, but OCR runtime is
  currently inactive in this environment (`tesseract` unavailable). Additional
  confidence gains depend on enabling that runtime path.
